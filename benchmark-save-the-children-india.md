# Save the Children India / Bal Raksha Bharat: website benchmark for Abhiara Foundation

**Organisation reviewed:** Save the Children India, presented on its official website as **Bal Raksha Bharat**.  
**Review date:** 26 September 2026.  
**Scope:** Only `balrakshabharat.org` and its official subpages were reviewed. This is a pattern benchmark, not a recommendation to reuse its wording, claims, branding, images, or design.

## Navigation and current-work structure

The site uses a broad, action-led information architecture. Its primary navigation separates **About Us**, **What We Do**, **Partnership**, **Get Involved**, and **Resources**, while a donation route remains prominent. The About group leads to organisational overview, mission, governing council, team, certificates, awards, and financials. The work group leads to strategy, presence, and thematic pages; the resources area directs visitors to blogs, news, press, reports, annual reports, FAQs, and other material. [1]

Current work is explained through two useful lenses. The programme directory lets a visitor narrow programmes by **theme** and **state**, while the education page gives a plain-language description of its methods, age groups, programme pillars, historic achievements, a dated cumulative reach figure, and a defined 2025–27 objective. [2] [3] This makes a large portfolio easier to explore without requiring a visitor to understand internal project names first.

For **Abhiara**, retain the underlying simplicity, not the scale:

- Use a short top navigation: **About**, **Education programme**, **Updates**, **Transparency**, **Donate**, and **Contact**. Keep a clearly labelled **Odia / English** switch in the header rather than splitting attention across many top-level areas.
- Make **Education programme** the only primary programme page. Put limited verified support on a smaller, clearly subordinate card such as **Verified support when needed**, explaining its eligibility, verification method, current capacity, and whether it is open or paused. Do not imply a broad multi-theme portfolio.
- Give each update an owner-selected status: **Current**, **Completed**, **Planned—not yet funded**, or **Archive**. Show a “last updated” date and programme period. This is realistic in a no-code CMS and prevents a young organisation from presenting proposals as delivery.
- Offer an **Updates** filter for current programme notes, then a separate **Archive** landing page for closed programme cycles, old announcements, and superseded documents. Do not bury old material in endless pagination.

## Trust, governance, and safeguarding

Bal Raksha Bharat makes governance visible rather than merely referring to it. The governing-council page identifies the chairperson, treasurer, and members and supplies role-labelled biographies. Its team page separately identifies senior staff roles, including finance/legal/IT and organisational development/governance. [4] [5] The financials page then offers year-selectable audited accounts, FCRA accounts, and annual reports, with older records available on request. [6]

Its public safeguarding presentation is especially concrete: it describes four safeguarding pillars, states a zero-tolerance position, offers an email address and phone route, and provides a reporting form that permits anonymous reporting and explains the scope of the channel. [7] The website also maintains a privacy policy that describes donation-related data collection, security claims, circumstances in which data may be shared, cookies, and a donor-support contact. [8]

For **Abhiara**, the small, credible version is more valuable than a long institutional façade:

- Build one **Trust & transparency** page with the Section 8 legal name, CIN, registered address, trustees/directors, date of incorporation, contact email, and the status of 12A, 80G, and FCRA **only as factually applicable**. If an approval is pending or unavailable, say so plainly; never use a trust badge to imply it exists.
- Publish a compact governance card for each trustee: name, office, relevant expertise, and conflict-of-interest declaration or policy link. Add a separate contact for governance or complaints.
- Publish a short child-safeguarding statement, code of conduct, reporting route, response expectations, and an emergency disclaimer. A protected form can allow anonymous concerns, but never expose submitted reports in the owner dashboard to unnecessary staff. Keep cases and attachments access-restricted.
- Include a short, specific privacy notice in English and Odia. Say what donor and form data are collected, why, who can access them, how long they are retained, and the contact for deletion/correction requests. Link the payment provider’s policy instead of making unsupported technical-security claims.
- In the no-code owner dashboard, maintain only a **document register**—title, type, period, approval date, public/private status, bilingual file/link, and review due date. This creates a low-effort compliance routine without exposing sensitive donor or child data.

## Field-story pattern and child-privacy implications

The success-stories index follows a repeatable publishing model: date, story category, title, short excerpt, read-more link, pagination, email signup, related resources, and links to other editorial formats. [9] A representative education story follows a recognisable narrative arc: a named child’s initial barrier, family context, a named intervention, practical support and follow-up, a quantitative before/after outcome, a forward-looking ending, social sharing, reviewer attribution, and related stories. [10]

The structure is useful because it connects a field problem to a specific response and a measurable change. However, it is **not safe to transplant unchanged**. The representative story publicly identifies a 13-year-old by full name and supplies a precise village, school, family and income context, and attendance trajectory. That level of identification is inconsistent with Abhiara’s strict child-privacy requirement, even if consent may have been obtained; the reviewed page does not demonstrate consent or minimisation details. [10]

For **Abhiara**, use a privacy-first “field update” template instead:

1. **What changed in the learning setting** — broad geography only (for example, block or district if safe), reporting month, and programme status.
2. **What Abhiara did** — activity, verified support where relevant, and the implementing partner/teacher/community role.
3. **Evidence** — an aggregated, non-identifying count or programme measure with its measurement period and a clear source note.
4. **Community perspective** — an adult educator/parent voice, or an anonymised/composite learner perspective only when safeguarding review permits it.
5. **What happens next** — label it as a confirmed next activity or a proposed plan.

No child’s face, name, exact school, home location, household income, medical detail, attendance record, or identifying combination of facts should be published by default. Require an owner-dashboard checklist before publishing: consent record ID (stored privately), child-safety review, photo permission status, name mode (none/pseudonym/composite), geography precision, English text, Odia text, reviewer, and expiry/review date. Treat translation as editorial work: publish the Odia version only after human review, rather than auto-translating a child-related story.

## Donation experience

The donation page is a content-rich conversion page. It pairs a giving call with an explanation of programme areas, delivery approach, impact figures, stories, FAQs, and tax-related information. It explicitly refers to both a one-off gift and recurring-donation management in different places, so its experience is designed for more than a one-time-only donor model. [11] The broader site also repeats a disclosure that donations may be allocated to areas of greatest need, provides an 80G statement, and makes donor-data assurances; its privacy policy adds that third parties can process payment-related services. [8] [11]

For **Abhiara**, do not copy the recurring model or a lengthy national-scale donation page. Use one short bilingual donation flow:

- Lead with a fixed label: **One-time donation / ଏକକ ଦାନ**. Do not show a monthly toggle, recurring FAQ, or language that could imply automatic renewal.
- Offer modest INR presets plus “other amount,” a simple explanation of the education programme, and one clear statement of how undesignated gifts are used. If restricted support is offered, state whether it is a preference rather than a promise and how reallocations are approved.
- Before payment, show the legal entity name, address, tax-benefit status as actually applicable, receipt timing, payment-provider link, refund/contact route, and privacy link. Request only the information needed for payment, receipt, and statutory compliance; collect additional communications consent separately and unticked.
- After payment, show a receipt/reference number, a brief confirmation, and a non-identifying programme update option. Do not promise a particular child, photograph, rank, or outcome in exchange for a donation.
- Keep the owner dashboard limited to donor name/contact, consent, donation reference, amount, payment status, receipt status, and campaign/source. Use role-based access, export only when needed, and never place card/bank credentials, child records, or safeguarding reports in the same dashboard view.

## Reporting, transparency, archives, and future plans

The site offers a strong document-retention pattern. Its annual-report page presents a reverse-chronological series from 2025–26 back to 2007–08, each with a direct viewing link. [12] Its financial hub separates audited financials, FCRA accounts, and annual reports, each with a year selector. [6] Research and advocacy material is put in reports/publications libraries, where the site includes longer and shorter versions of some research and keeps older policy, research, and strategic material accessible. [13] [14]

The website does attempt temporal distinction in its content: the education page labels a 2025–27 aim and groups achievements by past years, while an older strategy document is described with its 2022–24 period. [3] [13] Still, its resource libraries can mix current studies, historical publications, and expired strategies under broad “all documents” listings. The site therefore preserves archives well, but a visitor must sometimes infer whether a document is still current.

For **Abhiara**, make the distinction explicit and easy to maintain:

- **What we are doing now:** one current programme page and a quarterly update feed, each dated and status-tagged.
- **What we have completed:** a small archive of closed-cycle updates, annual reviews, and past campaign pages. Preserve links; add “archived” and the end date rather than deleting history.
- **What we plan next:** a separate “Next 12 months” page. Show only board-approved goals, dependencies, and whether funding is confirmed, partly confirmed, or sought. It must not sit beside impact evidence as if delivery had already occurred.
- **Transparency:** publish a yearly activity-and-finance update, signed or approved by the responsible governance body, plus downloadable governing documents as they become available. A young Section 8 can begin with a dated annual update, cash-receipts/expenditure summary at an appropriate level, programme outputs, independent verification notes where available, and a candid limitations section.
- In the dashboard, one document record should drive every public card: **type, reporting period, status, publication date, file/link, English/Odia availability, supersedes, and archive flag**. This is enough to enforce chronology without a custom system.

## What to borrow—and what not to borrow

**Transferable small patterns**

- A visible donate action and a small, stable top navigation. [1]
- Separate leadership/governance, financial records, safeguarding, and privacy into findable trust pages rather than hiding them in a footer. [4] [6] [7] [8]
- A repeatable update structure that moves from need to activity to dated evidence. [9] [10]
- Annual reports and accounts kept in chronological public archives. [6] [12]
- A documented distinction among completed work, active work, and time-bounded strategy/next steps. [3] [13]

**Do not copy**

- Names, slogans, impact claims, programme descriptions, layouts, photography, map, copy, awards, or global/national scale signals.
- A large multi-sector portfolio or state-filter interface when Abhiara has one core education programme and limited verified support.
- The representative story’s degree of child and household identification. [10]
- Recurring-giving cues, despite the site’s one-off-gift wording, because Abhiara offers one-time gifts only. [11]
- A resource page that mingles current plans and historical documents without an obvious status and period label. [13] [14]
- Generic assurances such as “secure” or tax-exempt without the supporting legal status, operational detail, and current review process.
