# Save the Children India / Bal Raksha Bharat campaign benchmark for Abhiara Foundation

## What the reference campaign does

The reference is the **Bal Raksha Bharat / Save the Children India** campaign page at `/stc-brand/`, read with the organisation’s relevant official donation, safeguarding, evidence, privacy, governance, and programme pages. This is a pattern benchmark, not a copy brief: no wording, figures, page designs, imagery, or brand devices should be reused.

The campaign uses a deliberately simple persuasion sequence. It begins with a child-centred education need, explains that support is used across an organisational fund, establishes longevity and public-sector collaboration, puts a donation module near the top of the page, and closes with a short impact reassurance plus legal, tax and data notes. Its message is intentionally broad: education leads the entry point, while health, protection and a safer future establish why flexible support is useful. [1]

A useful lesson is the **relationship between proposition and proof**, rather than the scale itself: practical problem → what the organisation does → why it can be trusted → one clear next step → terms that set expectations. The referenced organisation can support this with a long operating history, nationwide work, publicly listed evidence, a named governing council and audited records. [1] [4] [5] [11]

## Hierarchy and strongest campaign patterns

The campaign page is a conversion page rather than a complete organisational profile. It gives the visitor a specific reason to act on education, then directs them into the payment interaction, instead of asking them to navigate a large programme catalogue first. The dedicated donation page expands the same trust case with programme tabs, examples of support, frequently asked questions, impact stories, organisational geography, and further donation prompts. [1] [2]

The most transferable patterns are:

- **A focused lead cause.** Education is the entry point, even though the reference organisation operates across several sectors. This makes the initial ask easier to understand. [1] [2]
- **A clear donor proposition followed by operational context.** The campaign makes clear that support is assigned according to need rather than promising that a particular illustrated item will be bought for an individual child. That expectation-setting is important for unrestricted or flexible giving. [1]
- **Trust is distributed, not claimed in a badge.** The organisation links its work to public institutions, policies, programme pages, financial records, reports, safeguarding and a registered office. [1] [2] [4] [9]
- **Evidence has a destination.** A public reports library includes research and baseline studies, while annual reports and audited/FCRA materials are separately available. That lets a concise appeal page remain readable without making it the sole source of proof. [4] [5] [6]
- **Action is repeated without changing the task.** Donation links and the payment widget occur at decision points, while other actions such as volunteering are secondary. [1] [2]

## Trust, safeguarding and donor-data signals

The strongest reference pattern is treating safeguarding as a first-class public accountability page, not an obscure policy link. The policy states a four-part approach covering awareness, prevention, reporting and response; applies it across programmes and activities; includes staff, partners, volunteers, interns and other representatives; and says breaches can lead to employment, contractual or legal consequences. It also names country-level safeguarding focal points and carries a CEO statement of responsibility. [3]

The reporting route is concrete. The page offers email and telephone contact, an anonymous-reporting option, a structured incident form, a defined scope for complaints involving the organisation or its projects, and onward emergency or statutory directions for concerns outside that scope. This is a much stronger trust signal than a generic statement that children are important. [3]

For donor trust, the reference makes its registered-office contact visible, requests statutory information needed for tax treatment, links a privacy policy and terms, and states that payment-card/bank details are not stored in the website disclosure. Its privacy policy explains purposes for collection, third-party payment and communication vendors, security assertions, cookies, legal disclosure, and donor-support contact details. [1] [7] [8]

The reference also makes organisational accountability inspectable: it publishes a named governing council with member biographies, a senior team with roles and experience, an annual-report archive, audited financials, and FCRA accounts. [4] [5] [11] [12]

There is an important caution for Abhiara: the reference privacy policy permits a broad communications consent and sharing with vendors to conduct donor outreach. [7] Abhiara should not adopt that approach by default. Its donor data should be limited to what is necessary to process the one-time payment, issue an acknowledgement, meet legal obligations and answer a request. Marketing contact should be a separate, unticked opt-in in both English and Odia.

## Impact evidence and how claims are qualified

The reference does more than present an aggregate reach number. Its reports page links to underlying topical research, including baseline studies and policy/evidence reports. The education page distinguishes completed reach from an explicitly dated historical period, places a future goal in a named future period, identifies age groups, and attributes wider system statistics to government datasets. [6] [9]

Its annual-report page provides a long chronological archive, and its financials page separately exposes annual reports, audited accounts and FCRA accounts by year. This enables a visitor to trace assertions to a reporting cycle rather than take a campaign statistic on faith. [4] [5]

Its donation language contains a useful qualification: illustrative descriptions of what a gift can help make possible are not treated as a binding item-by-item promise; resources may move to the highest-need areas. [1] The donation page also uses some time-bounded programme figures, for example a health-and-nutrition number qualified to 2022. [2]

However, the current official pages show why claim governance matters. The campaign says the organisation is present in 26 states and Union Territories and has changed the lives of more than 11 million children through March 2026; the broader About page describes 19 states and three Union Territories and “over 1 crore” children; the donation page also gives 26 states/UTs, 60+ programmes and 11 million children. [1] [2] [13] These may reflect different definitions, reporting dates, or website updates, but the pages do not reconcile them. For a young organisation, even a small mismatch would reduce trust. Abhiara should assign an owner, a source, a definition, an as-of date, and a review date to every public number.

## Storytelling and presentation of people

The reference combines institutional credibility with personal narrative. A representative success story follows one young person through a barrier, programme participation, a setback and an employment outcome, then connects the individual account to a relevant public skills initiative. It is dated and has a named staff author with a role and short biography. [10] The donation page also routes visitors from impact statements to individual stories and describes child participation through young leaders. [2]

The transferable principle is **specificity with context**: show an activity, a barrier, a change observed, and the limits of the organisation’s role. Do not present donors as rescuers or imply that one payment single-handedly caused a life outcome.

For Abhiara, the reference story itself is not a privacy model to emulate. It includes a person’s name, locality, family bereavement, training institution, employer, pay and employment progression. [10] Abhiara’s strict child-privacy requirement means its site should never publish an identifiable child’s full name, exact village/school, household circumstances, face, voice, contact information, or a combination of details that allows re-identification. A child’s dignity and safety take priority over a more emotionally vivid campaign.

## Donation and action flow

The reference campaign uses an embedded payment widget in the main conversion area. The page configuration supports both one-time and committed gifts; the official widget script routes that configuration to the general giving interface, while the terms explicitly describe single and recurring transactions at selected frequencies. The donation FAQ also has a route to change a recurring gift. [2] [8] [14]

The reference provides several routes: online payment, bank transfer and cheque, with donor-support contact information; its FAQ says a donation receipt follows after processing. It explains the need for donor name, complete address and PAN where tax treatment is sought, and its pages state 80G eligibility. [1] [2] [8]

The strongest UX practices to adapt are:

- Put the **single action** beside the first explanation of what support enables.
- Say plainly whether support is flexible or programme-specific before payment.
- Keep a visible route to human support and a receipt expectation.
- Put privacy, payment-security information, terms, and statutory notices next to or immediately below the payment form—not only in a footer.
- Use questions that anticipate hesitation: what the payment supports, what information is collected, when an acknowledgement arrives, and how to contact the organisation. [2] [7] [8]

## Practical adaptations for Abhiara Foundation

### A credible first campaign

Build a single, owner-managed campaign template in **English and Odia**, with the same facts and an explicit language switch. Lead with a narrow education proposition that is true today, such as support for a stated learning activity, education material access, or verified educational assistance. Mention limited secondary support only in a subordinate “where support may also be needed” note, not as equal programme pillars. Do not claim national presence, systemic influence, large cumulative reach, or a transformation that cannot be evidenced.

Use this hierarchy:

1. **One locally grounded education need** and a concise explanation of Abhiara’s present response.
2. **What one-time support helps fund**, using “can help” language and a transparent flexibility/allocation note.
3. **Why trust us now:** Section 8 status, registered address, verified directors, programme geography only if verified, payment provider, privacy commitment, and a link to safeguarding.
4. **One-time payment module** with no recurring toggle or preselected subscription.
5. **What happens next:** acknowledgement timing, how questions are handled, and a simple statement that 80G approval is pending.
6. **Evidence and updates:** a link to an activity/evidence page with date-stamped, source-backed information.
7. **Safeguarding, privacy, contact, terms and complaints** in the footer and directly from the campaign page.

This is realistic for an owner-managed CMS if the page is built from reusable fields: English title/body; approved Odia translation; claim source URL or document; as-of date; claim owner; consent/expiry status for any media; and reviewer/publish date. The CMS should not permit publishing a statistic without a source, date and owner, or publishing a child-related image/story without a recorded safeguarding approval.

### One-time donation UX and tax honesty

Offer **only one-time payment**. Label it plainly in both languages and do not show an unavailable monthly option. Let the donor choose an amount or enter a custom amount. Collect only the fields needed by the payment provider, receipt process and applicable law. Give a clear acknowledgement window and a monitored support email/phone only if Abhiara can genuinely respond through it.

Place this status beside the amount and in the receipt flow: **“80G approval is pending. This contribution is not currently eligible for an 80G tax-deduction receipt.”** Do not say “tax exempt,” “eligible,” or imply that approval is assured. If the status changes, update the campaign, checkout, receipt text, FAQ and footer together, with the effective date and approval details checked by the responsible director.

Use an allocation statement suited to actual operations: a gift supports the stated education work; if a specific activity becomes impracticable, Abhiara will apply it to the closest verified education need or contact the donor where a restricted commitment requires it. Never promise that a set rupee amount delivers a particular item, attendance result, exam outcome, or individual child benefit unless Abhiara can fulfil and document that promise.

### Safeguarding and child privacy

Publish a short, practical **Child Safeguarding and Privacy** page before placing children at the centre of fundraising. It should cover conduct expected of directors, staff, volunteers, visiting partners and contractors; recruitment/screening and training appropriate to Abhiara’s actual capacity; reporting routes; response and escalation responsibilities; non-retaliation; emergency signposting; and the date, approver and next review date. The reference shows the value of a dedicated reporting channel, anonymous option, scope explanation and a named accountability structure. [3]

Do not claim a nationwide network of safeguarding focal points or a specialised investigation service before it exists. Instead, name the actual safeguarding lead or role, publish only a monitored reporting route, and say how urgent danger should be directed to emergency services/Child Helpline 1098 where appropriate. A safeguarding report form should request the minimum facts needed, not be mixed with the ordinary contact form, and should not promise anonymity or confidentiality beyond what Abhiara can provide.

Use **privacy-preserving evidence** as the default: aggregated activity logs; de-identified observations; a teacher, facilitator or adult community voice; anonymised child quotations only where safely approved; illustrated/composite scenarios clearly labelled as such; or photos of materials, learning spaces, hands, or adults where consent is valid. For any child material that might identify someone, require documented guardian consent, age-appropriate child assent where appropriate, a risk review, a time limit, withdrawal handling, and a decision not to reveal sensitive details. Avoid all donor recognition walls, individual child sponsorship displays, and automatically shared child stories.

### Evidence, reporting and people presentation

Start with a small public **Evidence & Updates** page rather than an inflated impact dashboard. Each quarterly or half-year update can state: period and location; activities completed; direct outputs supported by a register; what was learned or did not go as planned; next step; and links or descriptions of underlying records where publication is safe. Use counts only after checking duplicates and defining whether they represent attendances, materials distributed, sessions, households, or unique participants. State the denominator, timeframe and geographic scope beside every number.

Make a modest annual transparency note once records exist: board/director list and roles; registration facts; programme overview; total funds received and spent by broad category; material related-party disclosures if applicable; safeguarding summary without case details; and an accountant/auditor status stated accurately. Link audits, statutory filings and policies only when available. The reference’s separation of programme reports, annual reports and financials is a helpful information architecture to grow into, not a requirement to imitate at full scale on day one. [4] [5] [6]

Present people through accountable roles, not celebrity. A concise director/team page should list only verified names, roles, relevant qualifications, conflicts process, and contact/complaint routes. The reference’s named board and senior team make responsibility visible; Abhiara can adapt this as a short factual director page rather than long biographies. [11] [12]

## Patterns that are not suitable for Abhiara now

- **Scale-led persuasion:** Do not use large cumulative beneficiary figures, multi-state maps, dozens of programmes, or national-policy influence claims without current, published evidence. The reference can rely on substantial archives; Abhiara should build its evidence base first. [4] [5] [6]
- **Recurring-gift UX:** The reference supports committed and recurring gifts; Abhiara must offer one-time payments only. [8] [14]
- **80G language before approval:** The reference can display 80G treatment. Abhiara must prominently say that 80G is pending and that current gifts are not eligible.
- **Highly identifiable beneficiary stories:** Do not adopt stories that name a child/young person alongside exact locality, family adversity, school/employer, salary, image or timeline. [10]
- **A donor wall, sponsor-style child pages, or donor-to-child messaging:** These conflict with Abhiara’s privacy standard and are unnecessary for trust.
- **Broad WhatsApp/SMS marketing consent or vendor sharing by default:** Separate operational acknowledgements from optional communications; retain only the minimum donor data required. [7]
- **Huge outcome promises:** Do not suggest that one payment ensures enrolment, learning gains, safety, a career, or a change in a child’s life. Use qualified language and show verified work instead.
- **Borrowed authority:** Do not imply partnerships with governments, schools, experts, auditors, brands, or international networks until formally confirmed and publishable.
