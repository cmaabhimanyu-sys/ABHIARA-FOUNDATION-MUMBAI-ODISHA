# SATHI India benchmark: practical patterns for Abhiara Foundation

**SATHI India** is the only organisation reviewed for this benchmark. This review is based on SATHI’s live official website and official PDFs opened on **26 September 2026**. It is an observation of public presentation, not an assessment of programme quality, legal compliance, or the accuracy of self-reported figures.

## Navigation and information architecture

SATHI uses a broad header organised around four useful audience pathways: **Who We Are**, **What We Do**, **Get Involved**, and **Gallery/Blog**, plus a persistent **Donate** action. The “Who We Are” branch leads to identity, legal status, governance roles, management, recognitions and partners. “What We Do” separates current programmes, previous projects, annual/financial/legal reports and research. “Get Involved” distinguishes volunteering, fundraising and internships; the blog branch distinguishes articles, success stories and newsletters. This is a strong *information grouping* pattern, even though it is much wider than a young organisation needs. [1]

The home page quickly moves from mission to programme themes, story cards, partners and a donation call to action. It also uses simple impact counters and item-level examples of what a contribution can support. [2] For a first-time visitor, this makes the intended journey legible: understand the cause, see the work, read a case example, then give.

**What Abhiara can borrow, in a much smaller form:** use a five-item primary navigation—**About**, **Education**, **Updates**, **Transparency**, **Donate**—with **Odia | English** language choice in the utility bar. Put “Our support policy” under Education rather than giving limited verified support its own equal programme category. In the owner dashboard, make every page a structured record with title, status, language, date, short summary, hero image permission status, and publish date; this avoids a sprawling navigation and makes bilingual publishing realistic.

## Trust and governance presentation

SATHI makes several kinds of credibility evidence visible rather than leaving all legitimacy implicit: a concise organisational history and scale statement, a legal-status page, named Board Members with roles and short biographies, a separately labelled advisory board, named management staff, named partners/government allies on the home page, awards, annual reports, legal-document downloads and research publications. [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

Two small structural choices are especially transferable. First, the site distinguishes **Board Members**, **Advisors** and the **Management Team**, so a visitor can see that operational work and strategic advice are not presented as the same thing. [5] [6] [7] Second, its legal page provides a compact list of registration/compliance categories and the legal-documents page links selected certificates. [4] [8]

For Abhiara, publish a short **Trust & governance** page from day one, but only with verified facts: Section 8 legal name/CIN, registered office state, PAN and 12AB/80G only if actually approved, governing-board names and roles, conflict-of-interest statement, child-safeguarding contact, and a date showing when each disclosure was reviewed. Keep an “Advisor” label separate from “Board/Director”; do not imply governance authority where it does not exist. A simple dashboard register can power this page: document type, identifier (where safe to publish), issue/expiry date, public/private status, source file and reviewer.

The benchmark also shows a useful caution. SATHI’s published legal-status list contains categories such as PAN, 12A/80G, FCRA, CSR, TAN and Darpan, but not every item is accompanied by a public identifier on that page; its financial-reports page returned only a skip link in this review, while newsletters stated they would be updated shortly. [4] [10] [11] For a young organisation, a smaller disclosure set that is current, dated and actually downloadable is more trustworthy than a long menu with empty destinations.

## Field-story pattern and child privacy implications

SATHI’s story index is a card collection: each card uses a programme label, a descriptive headline, a person’s name and a brief location/context line, then links to a full story. [12] Full stories generally follow a human sequence: context and barrier, intervention actions, change or intended next step, then a wider learning/impact statement. The education story is particularly clear about the family’s migration-related barriers, the educational support proposed and the hoped-for next step. [13] The latest annual report similarly mixes an opening field case with programme sections, service data, studies, staff voices and governance/administration. [14]

This is a usable editorial template, but **not a privacy template for Abhiara**. The two public examples reviewed disclose a child/young person’s full name, age, school/college, locality, family circumstances and sensitive wellbeing information; one also reproduces direct quotations from a 15-year-old. [13] [15] Abhiara should not imitate that disclosure level.

**Safe Abhiara story card:** use a broad geography (for example, “coastal Odisha” or “Cuttack district” only where risk-assessed), programme period, the barrier, the support provided, a verified outcome or “next milestone,” and an optional staff/partner perspective. Use a pseudonym or composite by default; omit school name, precise village, family identifiers, health/trauma detail, route/location markers and identifiable child photos. Never publish a child’s voice, image or case detail merely because it is compelling. A dashboard story form should require: consent basis, safeguarding review status, anonymisation checklist, photo-release status, date for review/expiry and English/Odia versions before it can be published. If consent is unavailable or withdrawal is requested, use a non-identifying programme update instead.

## Donation experience

SATHI’s donation page makes the benefit case before the ask, states broad uses of funds, provides cumulative and project-level figures, and displays a “Scan & Pay” QR image. [16] The home page reinforces the request with a clear Donate action and illustrative support units. [2] The separate fundraising page invites personal, workplace and community fundraising. [17]

For Abhiara’s **one-time donations only**, use a deliberately simpler route: one prominent “Donate once” button; three optional predefined amounts plus “other amount”; a clear statement of the currently available payment methods; donor name/email/mobile fields limited to receipt and acknowledgement needs; a confirmation screen; and a receipt/thank-you email. Before payment, show the legal entity name, whether an 80G receipt can be issued and under what verified conditions, the refund/contact route, and a short data-use notice. Do not display a generic QR code without a visible payee name and donor follow-up path. Do not add a “monthly” toggle, recurring promise language, child sponsorship framing or unverified tax-benefit claim.

The dashboard can remain no-code: a donations sheet or database with payment reference, amount, date, donor contact, consent to updates, receipt status, acknowledgement status and reconciliation status. It should never expose donor records publicly, and access should be limited to authorised owners.

## Reporting, transparency and time boundaries

SATHI’s strongest pattern is **explicit time-based separation**. Its navigation gives Current Programs and Previous Projects distinct destinations. Current-project entries describe active service models and then use clearly forward-looking language such as what SATHI “envisions” or plans to expand. [18] Previous Projects is labelled as such and includes historical dates, completion language and past results. [19] A separate milestones page presents dated organisational history, while the current-programme page signals future expansion within individual programme descriptions. [20] [18]

Its annual-report page lists four year-labelled PDFs (2021–22 through 2024–25), and the latest report’s contents separate programme intervention, research, field voices, staff development and governance/administration. [9] [14] The research page also provides separately titled report downloads. [21] Together, these patterns give visitors different places for current activity, finished work, historic milestones and evidence.

Abhiara should make the distinction sharper with visible labels and a dashboard status field:

- **Now:** only active education activity, current locations stated at a safe level, actual delivery dates, and outcomes that are verified.
- **Updates:** dated field notes and anonymised stories, with “published on” and “last reviewed” labels.
- **Completed / archive:** closed pilots and past events, clearly dated; no current call to donate against a closed activity.
- **Next (planned):** a small separate section for intentions, each marked “planned—not yet funded/started” or “funded, starts [month/year]”; never phrase plans as delivered impact.
- **Transparency:** annual activity note, annual financial summary, governing-board disclosures, legal documents where appropriate, safeguarding/data privacy policy, and an archive by financial year.

For reporting, start with a modest annual **Activity & stewardship note** rather than simulating the scale of a mature annual report. It can state the reporting period; education activity delivered; number of learners/communities only where counted and safe; what support was provided; spending categories or a financial summary; named documents; methods/limits; and next-year plans clearly separated from results. Add an annual, board-approved financial statement/audit as required and only after it is ready. A disclosure page should show both the document year and upload date. This makes transparency durable while retaining a no-code publication workflow.

## Patterns to adapt—not copy

Do **not** copy SATHI’s language, design, programme categories, impact numbers, logo treatment, donation imagery, page names, or story structure word-for-word. SATHI works in child protection, rescue, restoration, shelters and several geographic programmes; Abhiara should make its own education-first scope unmistakable. [3] [18]

Do **not** import large cumulative counters or per-unit donation promises unless Abhiara has a defined calculation method, date range, source record and approval process. SATHI’s public pages use both headline and programme tables of cumulative figures, which is a mature-organisation approach that can become misleading if a young foundation’s denominator or timeframe is not obvious. [2] [16]

Do **not** use named child stories, precise locations, school details, family histories, health/substance-use information or identifiable child photographs as the engine of donor persuasion. Treat the benchmark examples as a reminder that story power and privacy protection must be designed together—not as a model for Abhiara’s child-data practice. [13] [15]
