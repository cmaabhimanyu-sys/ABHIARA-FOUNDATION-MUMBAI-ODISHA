# Pratham benchmark: small patterns for Abhiara Foundation

Pratham is a mature, national-scale education nonprofit, so **the useful lesson is its information architecture, not its scale, language, imagery, or claims**. The strongest transferable pattern is a clear path from what the organisation does now, to who is accountable, to proof and documents, to a simple donation action.

## Current navigation and information architecture

Pratham’s home page moves a visitor through four practical questions: who the organisation is, what programmes it runs, what recent reach it reports, and whose lives changed. The footer then repeats a compact set of durable destinations: story, board, leadership, work opportunities, partners, stories of change, publications and reports, contact details, social links, and privacy guidelines. Its programme pages also use an in-section menu, so a visitor can move between education areas without returning to the home page. [1] [2]

**Pattern worth adapting:** use a very small bilingual top navigation for Abhiara: **About / Our Education Work / Updates / Transparency / Donate**. Put an always-visible English–Odia switch in the header, not just in the footer. Keep “Support” as a secondary page rather than presenting it as co-equal with the education programme; label it clearly as a limited, verified support service. The owner dashboard should edit the label, English text, Odia text, status and publish date for each card—rather than allowing free-form menus.

A young organisation does not need Pratham’s multi-programme hierarchy. One “Education” landing page can contain the current activity, where it is happening, who it is for, the delivery approach, and a modest, dated progress indicator. Pratham’s elementary page makes its active method, delivery modes, assessment logic, and current-year reach findable in one place; that is a better model than a gallery of unverified claims. [2]

## Trust, governance and privacy presentation

Pratham places named governance and leadership pages in its About section. The board page distinguishes the national board from an emeritus board, and each entry gives a person’s role, a short qualification/context line, and a link to a fuller profile. The leadership page similarly names roles and backgrounds. [3] [4]

Pratham also has a legal-and-financial-information page that states its Section 8 registration, provides a finance contact, and links to a workplace sexual-harassment policy. Its site-wide footer gives office addresses, general contact details, social profiles and a dedicated privacy link. [5] [1]

Its privacy guidelines identify the kinds of website and donor data collected, purposes of use, security/safeguarding intent, a deletion/update route, a Data Protection Officer contact, and a breach-reporting route. [6] The donation form visibly asks for consent to a privacy notice and offers an anonymous-donation choice. [8]

**Pattern worth adapting:** create one plain-language **Transparency & safeguarding** page with only verified facts:

- legal name, Section 8 CIN/registration details where applicable, registered address, official email, and the names/roles of the actual directors;
- a dated, downloadable child-safeguarding policy; a complaints/speak-up contact; and a short conflict-of-interest statement;
- a concise privacy notice naming only the data Abhiara actually collects, a retention/deletion contact, and a clear statement that children’s identifiable data are not collected through public forms;
- financial-year documents and an update log as they become available, marked “Not yet available” rather than implied.

For Abhiara, governance photos are optional. A simple text card—name, statutory role, appointment month/year, and a one-sentence relevant background—is more realistic for a no-code dashboard and more useful than decorative portraits.

**Child-privacy guardrail:** do not transfer Pratham’s identifiable-person story convention. Abhiara should default to composite or adult/teacher/community stories; use first name/pseudonym, no precise village/school/class combination, no child face or voice, no family-income detail, no route/home location, and no before/after learner records. Any exceptional identifiable story needs written, revocable informed consent, a safeguarding review, and an expiry/review date. This goes beyond website-data privacy and should be enforced by the story publishing workflow.

## Field-story structure

Pratham keeps a dedicated, paginated “Stories of Change” index. Each preview has a participant-centred title, a one-sentence outcome-oriented teaser, and a link to the full article. [7] A recent story opens with the person’s present situation, explains the barrier to education, says when and how Pratham’s programme entered the story, briefly describes what the programme provides, follows milestones and then returns to the participant’s current direction. It supplies programme context rather than presenting the outcome as magic. [9]

**Pattern worth adapting:** give Abhiara one reusable, privacy-first update template:

1. **What we observed** — a de-identified learning or access need;
2. **What we did** — a dated activity, location only at safe district/block level, and the education objective;
3. **What changed or what we learned** — a modest, attributable observation; no causal overclaim;
4. **What comes next** — one bounded next action or review date;
5. **Privacy and verification note** — “Names/details changed to protect children,” consent status if an adult is identifiable, and the staff/board reviewer.

Make the public title descriptive rather than naming a child. In the dashboard, let the owner choose **Current update**, **Completed update**, or **Archive**; require a publish date and review date; and require English and Odia fields before publish. This preserves Pratham’s useful “story index → full context” pattern without turning beneficiaries into promotional content.

## Donation experience

Pratham’s donation landing page leads with an impact-oriented appeal, explains several programme areas, repeats a clear donation call-to-action, gives current-period reach figures, and links to supporting stories. The call-to-action takes the donor to a separate Pratham payment form. [10] The reviewed payment form asks for contact and tax-related details, amount, required privacy consent, optional marketing consent, anonymous donation preference, and payment method; it says contributions are eligible for 80G tax benefit. No recurring-frequency control was visible in this read-only entry form. [8]

**Pattern worth adapting:** Abhiara should make the route materially simpler and explicitly **one-time only**:

- a single “Donate once” button on every language version, leading to one trusted payment flow;
- three or four fixed suggested amounts plus “Other amount”; no monthly/recurring language, toggle, default, or post-payment upsell;
- just the minimum donor fields needed for receipt/compliance; separate optional update consent from the donation consent and default it to off;
- a pre-payment note stating the official account/payment processor, whether 80G is currently available, and when the receipt will arrive; and
- a clear success page with receipt/reference, donor-support contact, and return to current education updates.

Do not use a broad portfolio pitch when the verified programme scope is narrow. The page should say exactly what a one-time gift supports at the time of donation, distinguish **education programme** from **limited verified support**, and avoid assigning a donation to an individual child.

## Reporting, transparency, and present-versus-archive treatment

Pratham groups programme snapshots, annual reports, external/survey material, and other reports on one publications page. The annual-report list is chronological and preserves reports from 2007–08 through 2024–25, making the past accessible without pretending it is current. [11] Its live programme pages surface the current delivery model and current-year figures, while the partners page gives dated collaboration histories, period-labelled reach data and, in one case, an explicitly future-looking target. [2] [12]

**Pattern worth adapting:** use three visibly distinct content states on Abhiara’s site and dashboard:

| Public label | Meaning | Required dashboard fields |
|---|---|---|
| **Current work** | An activity occurring now or updated this quarter | start date, last verified date, status, short evidence note |
| **Reports & archive** | Closed activities and older annual documents | reporting period, publication date, PDF/link, archived flag |
| **Next steps** | A conditional intention, not a delivered result | target period, dependency/assumption, owner, “planned” label |

A lightweight quarterly **What we did / What we learned / Documents** update is sufficient at launch. Publish an annual report and independently reviewed/audited financial statements when available; do not manufacture an impact report before there is evidence. Keep prior updates and reports available in an archive rather than overwriting them. This borrows Pratham’s dated-document discipline while keeping claims proportional to Abhiara’s early stage.

For no-code operation, the dashboard needs only four structured collections: **Programmes**, **Updates**, **Documents**, and **People & policies**. Use controlled fields for status, period, language completion, privacy-review status, document type, and archive flag. Show a small “last verified” date on every public metric. A metric should not publish unless it has a period, source/reference, and owner review.

## What not to copy

- Do not reproduce Pratham’s phrases, page copy, visual treatment, campaign names, programme names, photos, case stories, numbers, donor messaging, or the look of its cards and calls-to-action.
- Do not imitate national-scale reach counters, extensive programme taxonomy, partner-logo carousel, or long leadership/board roster. They would overstate a young foundation’s operating maturity.
- Do not present future targets beside delivered outcomes without a strong “planned” label. The reviewed Pratham materials use both historical/current figures and future-facing targets, which is manageable at its scale but easy to blur on a small site. [12]
- Do not publish children’s names, faces, school identity, precise locality, learning level, health/disability/family context, quotations, or narratives merely because a large peer organisation may publish participant stories. Abhiara’s strict child privacy should be the stronger rule.
- Do not imply recurring giving, unrestricted eligibility for tax benefits, or the availability of support services that Abhiara cannot currently verify and deliver.
